The Driver Status panel, piece by piece

This is the panel on the right of the camera in the Streamlit app. It refreshes about 2.5 times a second. Below is an exact copy of it. Press a situation to watch the panel react the way the real app does, or hover any explanation to see which part of the panel it describes.

1

Status banner

The big coloured bar is the one-glance answer: is the driver OK right now? The number on the right is the drowsiness score from 0 to 100.

  • ALERT, score 0–39: awake and attentive.
  • WARNING, score 40–59: early signs of fatigue.
  • DROWSY, score 60–100, or an instant rule fired: it flashes and the alarm sounds.
  • NO FACE: nobody is in front of the camera.
  • STANDBY / CONNECTING…: the camera has not started yet.
2

Reason message

Shown only in WARNING or DROWSY. It names the single biggest reason, checked in this order:

  • 👁️ Eyes closed for X s: the eyes are shut longer than a normal blink (0.4 s).
  • 😴 Head falling forward / sideways for X s (alarm at 1.5 s): the head has dropped from its normal posture.
  • 🥱 Mouth open for X s (alarm at 2.5 s): a yawn is in progress.
  • 🥱 Frequent yawning: N in the last minute: 3 or more yawns.
  • 👁️ Eyes closed N% of the last minute: the PERCLOS measure is high.

Yellow box means a fatigue sign. Red box with 🔊 ALARM means the alarm fired, and it shows how many times it has fired this session.

3

Score gauge

The same score as the banner, drawn as a dial so you can see how close it is to the next level. The coloured bar grows from the left. The faint background bands are green 0–40, amber 40–60 and red 60–100.

The score is smoothed, so it rises and falls gradually instead of jumping on every frame. Once it reaches a band, it has to drop 6 points below that band's line before it leaves. That stops the colour flickering at the edges.

4

Eyes tile

Open or Closed. The top edge turns red while closed.

  • Eyes open: the small line shows EAR 0.30 · closed < 0.22 ✓. EAR is how open the eye is (lower = more closed). The second number is the line below which the eye counts as closed.
  • The ✓ appears after about 30 s, once the app has learned your normal eye shape and set that line for you.
  • Eyes closed: it switches to a stopwatch, 0.85s shut. At 1.2 s that is a microsleep and the alarm sounds immediately.
5

Mouth tile

Normal or Yawning. The top edge turns amber while yawning.

  • MAR 0.18 · yawn > 0.45: MAR is how wide the mouth is open. Above 0.45 counts as wide open.
  • It becomes Yawning only after the mouth stays wide open for 1.2 s, so talking and laughing are ignored.
  • Held open for 2.5 s: DROWSY and the alarm.
6

PERCLOS tile

The share of the last 60 seconds the eyes spent closed (normal blinks are not counted). This is the slow, long-term fatigue signal and the biggest part of the score (up to 40 points).

  • Under ~5%: normal.
  • Around 20%: clearly fatigued, worth the full 40 points.
7

Head tile

Upright or Down (forward / backward / sideways). The top edge turns red while the head is down.

  • pitch +3° · roll +2° ✓: pitch is the forward (+) or backward (−) lean, roll the sideways lean, both measured from your normal posture.
  • The ✓ appears after about a second, once the app has learned how you normally hold your head. Until then it says learning posture.
  • Head down means 20° forward, or 30° back or sideways. That gives at least WARNING straight away.
  • Down for 1.5 s: DROWSY and the alarm, even if the head falls so far the camera loses the face.

Numbers match config/config.yaml: blink 0.4 s, microsleep 1.2 s, yawn 1.2 s, yawn alarm 2.5 s, head drop 20° forward / 30° back or sideways, head alarm 1.5 s, WARNING at 40, DROWSY at 60. The player above uses illustrative readings that follow the same rules. For how the score itself is calculated, see docs/alert_guide.html.
