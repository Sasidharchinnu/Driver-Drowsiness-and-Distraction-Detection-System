Driver Drowsiness Detection · user guide

Why did the alarm go off?

The app watches your eyes and mouth through the webcam and turns what it sees into a drowsiness score from 0 to 100. The score decides which of three colours you see. This page explains, in plain words, exactly what raises the score and what sets off the alarm.

ALERTscore 0 – 39You look awake. Nothing to do.
WARNINGscore 40 – 59Early signs of tiredness. No sound yet.
DROWSYscore 60 – 100Danger. The alarm sounds.
NO FACEno scoreThe camera can't see your face.
The score

One number, three zones

Think of the score like a fuel gauge for tiredness. It sits near 0 while you drive normally and climbs when your eyes stay shut too long or you yawn. The two lines that matter are 40 and 60.

Why it doesn't flicker

Once you are in WARNING, the score has to fall below 34 before you go back to ALERT. Once in DROWSY, it has to fall below 54. Without this gap, a score of 39.9 → 40.1 → 39.9 would flash the colours back and forth.

What the camera measures

Two measurements: eye opening and mouth opening

First a face detector (YOLO) finds your face. Then a landmark model (MediaPipe FaceMesh) places 478 points on it. From those points the app measures two simple ratios, about 30 times per second.

Eyes: EAR (eye aspect ratio)

EAR = eye height ÷ eye width

A tall, open eye gives a big number. A closed eye is flat, so the number drops toward 0. The eye counts as closed when EAR falls below the threshold.

0.21

Everyone's eyes are different, so in the first second or two the app learns how open your eyes normally are and sets the threshold from that. A ✓ appears on the Eyes card once it has.

Mouth: MAR (mouth aspect ratio)

MAR = lip gap ÷ mouth width

Same idea for the mouth. Talking gives small, fast ups and downs. A yawn is a big opening that stays open. The mouth counts as wide open above 0.45.

Good to know

Your eyes naturally squeeze shut during a yawn. The app knows this and does not count eye closure while your mouth is wide open, so a yawn never sets off the microsleep alarm by accident.

Where the points come from

The score is made of four ingredients

Every frame, the app adds up four ingredients. Each one has a maximum, and together they can reach 100. The score then glides toward the new total over a fraction of a second, so one bad frame cannot spike it.

40points max

Closed-eye time in the last minute (PERCLOS)

The share of the last 60 seconds your eyes spent in closures longer than a blink. At 20% (12 seconds out of 60) it reaches the full 40 points.

Example: four slow 1-second blinks in a minute = 4 s = 6.7% → 13 points.

35points max

How long your eyes are closed right now

Zero for the first 0.4 s (a normal blink), then rising steadily to 35 points at 1.2 s. At 1.2 s the microsleep rule above takes over anyway.

Example: eyes shut for 0.8 s → halfway → 17.5 points.

15points max

Yawns in the last minute

1 yawn = 3.75, 2 yawns = 9, 3 or more = 15 points, plus 3 while you are yawning. On top of this, a yawn in progress or 3 yawns in a minute always lift the score into WARNING.

One yawn is normal. Several in a minute is a strong sign of tiredness.

10points max

Unusual blink rate

Healthy is 8 – 28 blinks per minute and adds nothing. Blinking much less (staring) or much more (fighting sleep) adds up to 10 points. Only checked after the first 30 s, when there is enough data.

Example: 4 blinks per minute → half the way to zero → 5 points.

The key point

Normal blinking adds 0 points to every ingredient. Only closures longer than 0.4 s, yawns, and an unusual blink rate move the score.

The alarm

When the sound actually plays

  1. The status must be DROWSY. WARNING never makes a sound; it only changes the colour.

  2. For a microsleep or a long yawn, the alarm plays immediately. For DROWSY reached through the score, it must last 0.6 s first, so a single glitchy frame can't set it off.

  3. After it plays, it waits at least 3 s before it can play again, so it doesn't stack up.

Where you hear it

When you run the app on your own computer, the alarm plays through that computer's speakers. When the app runs on a server, choose This browser in the sidebar under Alarm. Use Test alarm to check the volume.

Adjusting it

If it doesn't fit your face

  • !

    Yawns are missed: open your mouth wide and read MAR on the Mouth card. Set Yawn above (MAR) a little below that number.

  • !

    Alarm on a long yawn feels too quick or too slow: change Alarm when mouth open for (s) (default 2.5 s).

  • ✓

    Blinks flagged as closed: keep Auto-calibrate to my eyes on and look normally at the camera for two seconds after pressing Start.

  • ✓

    Microsleep alarm too sensitive: raise Micro-sleep after (s) above 1.2 s.

All of these are in the sidebar and take effect immediately. The defaults live in config/config.yaml.

Words used in the app

Glossary

EAR
Eye aspect ratio. Eye height divided by eye width. Small means closed.
MAR
Mouth aspect ratio. Lip gap divided by mouth width. Large means wide open.
PERCLOS
"Percentage of eye closure": the share of the last minute your eyes spent in closures longer than a blink. Road-safety research uses it as one of the best tiredness measures.
Microsleep
Falling asleep for a second or two without noticing. At 100 km/h a car travels about 33 metres in one second.
Calibration
The app learning how open your eyes normally are, so the "closed" line fits you.
YOLO
The face detector. It finds where your face is in each camera frame.
FaceMesh
Google's MediaPipe model that places 478 points on the face, including the eyelids and lips.
