Abstract

This project is a real-time driver monitoring system that runs on a live webcam. YOLO finds the driver's face, MediaPipe FaceMesh places 478 landmarks on it, and from those points the system measures eye closure (EAR), mouth opening (MAR), and head pose (pitch and roll). A state machine turns these measurements into a 0–100 drowsiness score and one of three states: ALERT, WARNING or DROWSY. An audible alarm sounds for a microsleep (eyes shut ≥ 1.2 s), a long yawn (mouth open ≥ 2.5 s), a falling head (head down ≥ 1.5 s), or a score of 60 or more held for 0.6 s. This report explains every metric, the formula behind it, the exact time at which each alarm fires, and the head-falling feature added in this revision.

1Introduction and objective

Driver fatigue causes a large share of road accidents because a sleepy driver loses reaction time well before falling fully asleep. At 100 km/h a car covers about 28 metres every second, so a 2-second microsleep means more than 50 metres driven blind.

The objective of this project is to detect the visible signs of fatigue from a camera facing the driver, without any wearable sensor, and to warn the driver early enough to matter. The signs monitored are:

  • Eye closure: long blinks, microsleeps, and the share of time the eyes are closed (PERCLOS).
  • Yawning: the mouth held wide open for a sustained period.
  • Blink rate: blinking that is unusually slow or unusually fast.
  • Head falling: the head dropping forward, backward or sideways as the neck muscles relax.

Everything runs on the live camera. There is no image or video upload and no manual input.

2System pipeline

Every camera frame passes through the same seven stages, in order. The code for each stage is in the src/ folder.

  1. Face detectionYOLO11n, fine-tuned on WIDER FACE, finds face boxes. Confidence ≥ 0.35, NMS IoU 0.45, input 480 px. detector.py
  2. Pick the driverThe largest face is the driver. Other faces are labelled as passengers and ignored.
  3. LandmarksThe face box is enlarged by 15% on each side and cropped. FaceMesh places 478 3D landmarks on the crop. landmarks.py
  4. MeasurementsEAR, MAR, head pitch and head roll are computed from the landmarks.
  5. ScoringThe engine tracks blinks, yawns, head drops and PERCLOS over time and outputs the score and state. drowsiness.py
  6. Heatmap and overlayGaussian blobs over the eyes and mouth glow hotter as they close or open. Boxes and the HUD are drawn. heatmap.py, overlay.py
  7. AlarmA two-tone siren plays on a background thread, so the video never pauses. alarm.py

Cropping the face before FaceMesh (stage 3) improves accuracy, because the eyes cover far more pixels in a face crop than in the full 640×480 frame. It also makes sure the measurements always come from the face YOLO chose as the driver.

3Metrics and how they are calculated

Each metric below gives the formula, the landmark points used, and the thresholds as they are set in config/config.yaml.

3.1 Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR)

per frame

EAR measures how open an eye is. It uses six landmarks: two eye corners (p1, p4) and two pairs of points on the upper and lower eyelids.

EAR = ( |p2 − p6| + |p3 − p5| ) / ( 2 × |p1 − p4| ) eyelid gap (vertical) ÷ eye width (horizontal) final EAR = (left EAR + right EAR) / 2

Dividing by the eye width makes EAR independent of distance. Leaning toward the camera makes the eye bigger but does not change the ratio.

Open eye0.25 – 0.35
Default closed thresholdEAR < 0.21
Confirmation2 frames in a row
Reopen hysteresisthreshold + 0.02

3.2 Personal EAR calibration

adaptive

Open-eye EAR differs between people. Some drivers sit near 0.22 with eyes open, others near 0.35, so one fixed threshold either misses closures or raises false alarms. The engine learns the driver's own open-eye EAR from the last 30 seconds and sets the threshold relative to it:

baseline = 80th percentile of open-eye EAR samples (last 30 s) threshold = clip( baseline × 0.72 , 0.12 , 0.30 ) active after 45 samples (~1.5 s at 30 FPS); before that, 0.21 is used

An eye counts as closed below the threshold, and as open again only above threshold + 0.02. This gap stops the state from flickering when EAR sits right on the line.

3.3 Closure duration: blink vs microsleep

timed

A blink and a microsleep both close the eyes. The system tells them apart only by how long the eyes stay shut, timed from the first confirmed closed frame.

Eyes closed forClassified asEffect
≤ 0.40 sNormal blinkCounted. Adds nothing to the score.
0.40 – 1.20 sLong, heavy blinkCounts toward PERCLOS. The closure channel rises from 0 to 1.
≥ 1.20 sMicrosleepDROWSY immediately, alarm with no extra delay.
closure_channel = clip( (closure_time − 0.40) / (1.20 − 0.40) , 0 , 1 )

3.4 Mouth Aspect Ratio (MAR) and yawning

per frame + timed

MAR uses the same idea for the mouth: the average lip gap at three points divided by the mouth width.

MAR = mean( |81−178|, |13−14|, |311−402| ) / |61 − 291| 61, 291 = mouth corners · (81,178) (13,14) (311,402) = upper/lower lip pairs

Talking also opens the mouth, but only in short bursts. To ignore talking, MAR is smoothed (MAR_s ← MAR_s + 0.15 × (MAR − MAR_s)) and the mouth must stay open for a minimum time before it counts as a yawn.

ConditionResult
Smoothed MAR > 0.45 for ≥ 1.2 sYawn counted, WARNING
Mouth held open ≥ 2.5 sDROWSY and alarm
3 or more yawns in the last 60 sAt least WARNING
Yawn endsMAR below 80% of the threshold for more than 0.4 s

The eyes naturally squeeze shut during a yawn, so eye closure is not scored while a yawn is in progress.

3.5 PERCLOS

rolling 60 s

PERCLOS (percentage of eyelid closure) is the most widely used fatigue measure in driver-safety research. It is the fraction of recent time the eyes were closed. Only closures longer than a normal blink (0.4 s) are counted, so ordinary blinking never raises it.

PERCLOS = Σ (long closure time in last 60 s) / max( min(elapsed, 60 s), 30 s ) perclos_channel = clip( PERCLOS / 0.20 , 0 , 1 ) 20% closed time = fully fatigued

The divisor is never less than 30 s. Without that floor, two blinks in the first three seconds of a session would look like a very high closure rate.

3.6 Blink rate

rolling 60 s

A healthy blink rate is about 8–28 blinks per minute. Fatigued drivers either stare (too few blinks) or blink rapidly while fighting sleep (too many).

blink_rate = blinks in last 60 s / (rate window in minutes) if rate < 8 : blink_channel = (8 − rate) / 8 if rate > 28: blink_channel = (rate − 28) / 28 otherwise : blink_channel = 0 ignored for the first 30 s

3.7 Head pose: pitch and roll

new in this revision

FaceMesh gives each landmark a depth value z as well as x and y. Smaller z means closer to the camera. The system takes the line from the forehead (landmark 10) to the chin (landmark 152) and measures two angles:

pitch = atan2( z_chin − z_forehead , y_chin − y_forehead ) + = head falling forward roll = atan2( x_chin − x_forehead , y_chin − y_forehead ) ± = leaning sideways z is scaled by the crop width so it is in the same units as x and y

When the head falls forward, the forehead moves toward the camera and the chin moves away, so z_chin − z_forehead grows while the face's height on screen shrinks. The pitch angle rises steeply.

Personal posture. Every camera sits at a different angle; a laptop camera below the face sees a different "upright" pitch than a dashboard camera. The engine therefore learns the driver's upright pose as the median pitch and roll over the last 30 s (once it has 30 samples, about 1 s), using only frames where the head is clearly upright. Every judgement uses the angle relative to that posture.

Forward drop≥ 20°
Backward drop≥ 30°
Sideways drop≥ 30°
Alarm after1.5 s down

4The drowsiness score

One frame says almost nothing, because everyone blinks and everyone opens their mouth. Drowsiness is a pattern over time, so the four channels from Section 3 are each normalised to 0–1 and combined with fixed weights:

raw score = 100 × ( 0.40 × perclos + 0.35 × closure + 0.15 × yawn + 0.10 × blink ) yawn channel = {0 yawns: 0, 1: 0.25, 2: 0.60, 3+: 1.0} + 0.20 while yawning (max 1.0)

The raw score is then raised to a minimum value (a floor) when a yawn or a head drop is in progress, so the gauge always agrees with the state. While the head is down, the floor climbs from 42 up to 58 over the 1.5 s before the alarm.

smoothed score = 0.25 × raw score + 0.75 × previous smoothed score
Smoothed scoreStateMeaning
0 – 39ALERTDriver is attentive
40 – 59WARNINGEarly signs of fatigue
60 – 100DROWSYAlarm zone
no faceNO FACENobody in view. The score decays by 5% per frame.

Hysteresis: once in WARNING or DROWSY, the score must fall 6 points below the band's lower edge to leave it. This stops the label from flickering when the score sits on a boundary.

Worked example

Suppose PERCLOS is 10%, the eyes are currently shut for 0.8 s, there was 1 yawn in the last minute, and the blink rate is 5 per minute.

perclos = 0.10 / 0.20 = 0.500 closure = (0.8 − 0.4) / 0.8 = 0.500 yawn = 1 yawn = 0.250 blink = (8 − 5) / 8 = 0.375 score = 100 × (0.40×0.500 + 0.35×0.500 + 0.15×0.250 + 0.10×0.375) = 100 × (0.200 + 0.175 + 0.0375 + 0.0375) = 45.0 → WARNING

5When the alarm sounds

There are two kinds of trigger. Timed events (microsleep, long yawn, head down) are already confirmed by their own duration, so the alarm plays the moment the time is reached. A high score must stay at DROWSY for 0.6 s first, so a single noisy frame cannot set it off.

Alarm behaviour

  • Sound: a synthesised siren that switches between 880 Hz and 1200 Hz six times per second, lasting 1.2 s, at volume 0.8. A changing pitch is harder to ignore than a constant tone.
  • Cooldown: at least 3 s between two sounds, so the alarm repeats every 3 s while the driver stays drowsy.
  • Visual: a red border and a "WAKE UP" banner on the video. The banner reads "HEAD FALLING" when the head is the cause.
  • Non-blocking: the sound plays on a separate thread, so the camera feed keeps running.

6New feature: head-falling detection

The original system watched only the eyes and mouth. A driver whose head drops onto their chest may still be missed if the eyes are hidden from the camera. This revision adds head-pose tracking so a falling head raises the alarm directly.

Detection logic

  1. Measure pitch and roll every frame (Section 3.7) and subtract the learned upright posture.
  2. The head counts as down when it is ≥ 20° forward, ≥ 30° backward, or ≥ 30° sideways. Backward tilt is ignored during a yawn, because the head tips back naturally then.
  3. While the head is down, the state is at least WARNING. After 1.5 s it becomes DROWSY and the alarm sounds immediately.
  4. The head counts as up again only when it returns within 80% of the threshold for more than 0.3 s. This prevents flicker from noisy frames.
  5. Head drops lasting 0.5 s or more are counted as head drops in the session statistics.

When the face falls out of view

A fully fallen head often leaves the camera's view, and FaceMesh can no longer find the face. Without special handling, the system would report NO FACE and stay silent at the most dangerous moment. To prevent this, if the face disappears within 0.8 s of the head being at least half-way down, the system treats the head as still down. The alarm sounds at the 1.5 s mark and keeps repeating for up to 8 s. A driver who simply leaves the seat does not lean forward first, so that case still shows NO FACE with no alarm.

9Conclusion and future work

The system detects the main visible signs of driver fatigue in real time on an ordinary laptop: microsleeps, long blinks, PERCLOS, abnormal blink rate, yawning, and now a falling head. Combining time-based rules (which react within 1.2–2.5 s) with a smoothed multi-channel score (which captures slower build-up of fatigue) gives fast alarms for dangerous events and few false alarms from normal blinking or talking. Per-driver calibration of both eye opening and head posture lets the same settings work across different faces and camera positions.

Future work

  • Use an infrared camera so the system works at night and through sunglasses.
  • Train the custom 5-class YOLO model (face, eye open, eye closed, mouth open, mouth closed) so eyes and mouth are detected in a single pass.
  • Add gaze direction to detect distraction (looking at a phone) as well as fatigue.
  • Run on an embedded board (Raspberry Pi or Jetson Nano) mounted in a vehicle.
  • Evaluate on a public drowsiness dataset (for example NTHU-DDD) to report precision and recall.
